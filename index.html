About me

I’m an ML researcher interested in reliable, unsupervised methods for finding unusual structure in medical images. I recently completed my PhD in Computing at Imperial College London, advised by Prof. Bernhard Kainz in the BioMedIA group. My work looks at self-supervised learning and generative models — especially diffusion and rectified flow — that can localise anomalies without labelled pathologies.

Background

I started at Imperial with an MEng in Computing (2017–2021), including a master’s project on machine learning for Crohn’s disease. In October 2021 I joined BioMedIA for a PhD on anomaly detection in medical imaging, finishing in 2025. During that time I interned at Meta and Google Research, and worked on synthetic-task training, reconstruction-based detection, and image-conditioned generative models that restore a pseudo-healthy image so the residual can be read as an anomaly map. Code for this work is on GitHub.
